Science is a battle, the days of publish-or-perish are now considered the good old times when everything was still fine. Today researchers have to publish several papers a year, same as before, but additionally foster a strong media presence to elevete your perceived importance during hireing procedures. A consequnce of this, old and new standards, is poor science. Poor science is also as easy to get through peer review as it ever was, thousands of papers are published daily, there aren't enough qualified experts to review all that is written. Many of which should not be send for review in the first place.

In this climate, I have been working for a mid-career scientist at Stockholm University, who had yet to score a permanent position. He had limited understanding of math and physics, often showing his ignorance in our discussions. But, that alone is no issue, scientist are experts at learning. They do not need to know everything from the get-go, they can figure out how things work when they need to. This one, however, didn't care, he had his eyes set on scoring a position, not aiming to answer scientific questions truthfully.

In our collaboration, I was tasked with an analysis of ocean watermass transports based on a very advanced methodology. Lagrangian tracking. Mathematically, the Lagrangian approach to watermass tracking is rather simle. The relevant equation is simply $\chi = f(\tau, \vect{a})$. The equation simply states that if I take an initial point, and then follow a watermass, then I can describe all processes along the way, knowing relative time and initial position alone.
